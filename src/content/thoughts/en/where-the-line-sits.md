How has the concept of privacy changed over the years? Is privacy a right? If yes, since when? How do culture and religion shape what privacy means? Who decides what counts as private? And why does privacy even matter in the first place?

I think to myself sometimes, how does society decide where to draw the line? That arbitrary line we collectively agree not to cross. What baffles me most is how the line keeps shifting its position with each cultural, tech-inspired shift.

Take the "since when" question. As far as I can tell, privacy as a right is younger than I expected. In 1890 two American lawyers, Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis, argued for "the right to be let alone", worried that "instantaneous photographs and newspaper enterprise" had invaded private and domestic life.[^1] The Universal Declaration of Human Rights wrote it down in 1948: no one should be subjected to arbitrary interference with their privacy, family, home or correspondence.[^2] So a new device or a nosier press shows up, people feel something being taken, and only then does someone give it a name. Was it there before it had a name, or did naming it make it real?

Then there is culture and religion. In Arabic, the word for the sacred and the forbidden (haram) and the word for the private quarters of a home (harim) grow from the same root, ḥ-r-m.[^3] Privacy there isn't only about me. It's about the household, the family, what is owed to the people inside the walls. That feels very different from privacy as something I own and can trade away in a terms and conditions box nobody reads. Which one is closer to the truth? Maybe neither. Maybe both, on different days.

And the line keeps moving. Not long ago, posting a photo of your dinner for strangers to see would have been strange. Now it's ordinary. We hand our location, our faces, even our heartbeat to apps, and still close the curtains at night. I do it too. So which of those is me deciding what's private, and which is just habit?

Maybe nobody draws the line. Maybe it's the sum of a million small shrugs. Or maybe it was never about the line, but about who gets to stand on which side of it.

I don't know yet. If the line keeps moving, does that make privacy weaker, or just alive? And the next time it moves, will I notice in time to ask whether I agreed?

[^1]: Samuel D. Warren and Louis D. Brandeis, ["The Right to Privacy"](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mac/classes/6.805/articles/privacy/Privacy_brand_warr2.html), *Harvard Law Review* 4, no. 5 (15 December 1890).
[^2]: [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), Article 12, adopted 10 December 1948.
[^3]: ["Ḥ-R-M"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-R-M), Wikipedia.
